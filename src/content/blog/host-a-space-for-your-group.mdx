If you already use Harvous for personal prep, Shared Spaces are the group layer—not a new product to learn from scratch.

Here’s the short version of how I’d host one for a class or small group.

---

## Create the space

Open Harvous and create a shared space for the group. Name it after the people or the series—“Wednesday James,” “Room 12,” “Young adults spring”—not after the app.

You’re hosting. Hosting is included with [Harvous Plus](/pricing/). Your members don’t need Plus to join.

If you’re still deciding whether a shared trail is even the problem you have, read [Keep what the group finds](/blog/keep-what-the-group-finds) first. This post assumes you already want the room.

---

## Invite your people

Send the invite link the same way you’d send a GroupMe or text: one link, clear label, low pressure.

Joining is free. That’s intentional. You shouldn’t have to sell a subscription for someone to add a question to week three.

You stay in control of who belongs. If someone leaves the group, they can leave the space. You’re not building a permanent parish directory—you’re holding study for *this* season.

---

## One thread per week

This is the habit that makes the space useful.

Don’t dump everything into one megathread called “notes.” Make a thread for the week or topic:

- “James 1 — week 1”
- “Sermon follow-up: Hebrews 4”
- “Easter class — week 3”

Then add what the room finds *there*. Questions. A scripture reference someone brought up. The one sentence you don’t want to lose.

<mark>The thread becomes the week’s record</mark>—same idea as personal [threads](/blog/start-with-threads), just shared.

When someone misses a night, they can catch the trail without asking you to recap from memory.

---

## Keep prep private

Your personal notes stay yours.

Prep the lesson in your own folders the way you already do. When something is ready for the group—an opening question, a passage aim, a follow-up—add *that* to the shared space.

The boundary matters: <mark>private prep stays private</mark> until you choose to share it. Members see what belongs in the space, not your half-formed Tuesday scribbles.

That’s how you keep the tool from turning into performance.

---

## Keep it light

A Shared Space fails the same way a Drive folder fails: when it becomes homework.

Aim for less than you think:

- One thread per gathering
- A few keepers, not a transcript
- An open invitation for members to add one line between weeks if something follows them

You’re not replacing conversation. You’re giving conversation a place to land.
