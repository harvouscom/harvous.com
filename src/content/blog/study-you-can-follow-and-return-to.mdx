We've been thinking about what an app should show you in the first second.

For most of Harvous's life the answer was an empty page. You arrived, and the app waited. Everything you had already done was in there somewhere—behind a column, under a heading, a click or two away—and the work of remembering it was yours.

<mark>Harvous 3 opens on your study.</mark> Not a blank note. The days you actually had.

That one decision is most of the release.

---

## An app that opens on your study

An empty page is a fair start for a notes app, because a notes app assumes the note you want is the one you're about to write.

Bible study runs the other way. The valuable thing is usually behind you: the passage you sat with on Tuesday, the phrase you marked in March and meant to come back to, the question a sermon left open that you half-answered a week later. If the app opens on nothing, it has quietly handed the remembering back to you—which is the one job you came to it with.

So Harvous now opens on **Activity**: what you read, what you marked, what you wrote, with the days behind it still there to look through.

## Follow: one sheet per day

The unit is a day, and every day gets a sheet.

A sheet says what that day held—the passages, the count, the notes you started. The days behind it stack up so a week is something you can look at rather than reconstruct. Over a few months that gives you a shape: a dense fortnight in Romans, a quieter April, a cluster in June where the same three chapters keep returning because you were building something around them.

<mark>The days you rested stay too.</mark> A day with nothing in it still gets its sheet, and the sheet says so plainly. We wrote about that decision separately in [the quiet days stay](/blog/the-quiet-days-stay)—the short version is that hiding the quiet stretches gives you a more flattering month and a fictional one, and the gaps turn out to be half the information.

## Return: what fades comes back

Following is only half of it. Something has to bring back the thing you would otherwise walk past.

That's **[Suggestions](/features/suggestions/)**: a note going cold, a highlight with no thought attached yet, a passage you've circled three times without writing anything down. They arrive when they're worth another look, and they come from what you already saved.

The name changed in this release. It used to be Recall, which described half the job—some of these bring something back, and some of them propose something new to make from what's already there. Suggestions covers both honestly. Old links still land where they should.

For people who asked for something more deliberate than waiting to be reminded, there's now **[Review](/add-ons/review/)** on Harvous Plus: a short question drawn from your own note, highlight, or verse. You write what you remember first, then say how it went—held it, mostly had it, or needed the reminder. <mark>Nothing is scored.</mark> How you answer decides when it comes back, and the gap widens as you hold it.

## Three views and a spotlight

Making room for Activity meant giving something up, and what went was the sidebar.

It had been doing four jobs in one narrow column—recent study under My Home, what to revisit, every organised view, and search—each getting the same cramped glimpse whether a glimpse was the right amount or not. Splitting those jobs by what they were worth in screen left three views you can hold in your head, left to right in the top left: Activity, the Bible reader, and a note.

Finding things went the opposite direction. It's now Search—a spotlight you summon from the top middle—with everything you've saved underneath it: notes, folders, threads, highlights, scripture, resources. It opens over your work and the note underneath stays exactly where it was. The longer version of that reasoning is in [the sidebar did too much](/blog/the-sidebar-did-too-much).

## What stayed the same

Everything you wrote is where you left it.

<mark>Your notes, highlights, threads, and folders carried over untouched</mark>—same scripture pills, same margin marks, same connections, now reachable from Search instead of the column. There's nothing to redo, migrate, or re-file. The reader we shipped in August is unchanged and still the place your notes sit beside the chapter.

---

That reader was the first piece of this direction, though it looked like a standalone feature at the time—[why Harvous has a reader now](/blog/why-harvous-has-a-reader-now) makes the case. This release is the rest of it.

The goal has been the same the whole way through, and I've written it down before in [remembering is the point](/blog/remembering-is-the-point): someone remembers their Bible study. A trail you can follow, and something that brings you back to it. Harvous 3 is the shape that serves it.
