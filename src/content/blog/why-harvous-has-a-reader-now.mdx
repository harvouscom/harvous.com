For a long time, if you asked what Harvous wasn't, the answer was easy: a Bible reader.

I said it on the homepage. I said it in the FAQ. I meant it—Harvous was never trying to be the app you open to read a chapter. It was the place you go after, to keep what you noticed.

That's still true. But this week Harvous shipped something that looks, on the surface, like it contradicts all of that: a reader. Browse the canon, open a chapter, and read it—full screen, eleven translations, cached for when you don't have a signal.

So did the "not a reader" thing stop being true? Not exactly. Here's what actually changed.

---

## What "not a reader" was really protecting

The claim was never really about the reading experience. It was about the center of gravity.

Plenty of good apps exist for reading a chapter—<mark>YouVersion, Olive Tree, your church's app</mark>. I didn't want to compete with any of them on reading plans, audio Bibles, or a community feed. That's a different product, built by people who are better at it than I'd be starting from scratch.

What I wanted to build was somewhere your notes could live—linked to the verse, threaded to the study, findable in six months. That's still the whole point of Harvous. Nothing about the reader changes it.

---

## Why add a reader, then

Here's the thing I kept running into: people would open Harvous, type a reference to start a note, read the verse in the pill—and then switch to another app to actually read the chapter around it. Then switch back to write. Then sometimes forget what they were about to say.

The problem was never that Harvous lacked a reading feature. It's that the switch broke the thing Harvous is actually for: staying in the text long enough to notice something.

So the reader isn't a new destination. It's <mark>the same notes experience, entered from a different door</mark>. Open a chapter and the margin already shows you which verses you've written about. Highlight a phrase and it's the same highlight you'd make from a note—one highlight layer, not two. Start writing mid-chapter and you never left the page to do it.

---

## What it isn't

It isn't a reading plan. It isn't an audio Bible. It isn't a community feed, a devotional, or anything that competes with the app you already trust for that. If YouVersion is where you build a daily reading habit, keep using YouVersion.

Harvous's reader exists for one reason: so that when you're already here—reading, writing, thinking—you don't have to leave to keep going.

---

## The short version

Harvous is still a notes app. It always will be. The reader didn't change that; it closed a gap between reading and writing that shouldn't have existed in a notes-first product in the first place.
