When we built My Family, the question I kept coming back to was simple. What should a parent see?

A teen's Bible study is theirs. It might be the most honest writing they do. But a parent also wants to know whether their kid is reading at all. So Harvous shows <mark>progress, never content</mark>, and it shows the teen the same numbers.

Younger children are different, and I'll get to them below.

---

## Teens 13 and up

A teen in your family has their own Harvous account. If you're a parent or guardian in the family, you can see:

- when they were last in Harvous
- how many chapters they've read, and which books
- how many notes they've written

You can't see their notes, their highlights, what they search for, or their Review. Not a summary of them, not a preview. Just the counts.

The teen sees the same numbers about themselves, so nothing is watched in secret.

---

## Adults in the family

Nobody sees an adult's progress. Adults share the Family Space and Harvous Plus, and their study stays theirs.

---

## The Family Space

Anything someone adds to the Family Space, everyone in the family can see. That's the point of it. Nothing moves there on its own: a note only goes in if someone puts it there.

Our Bible shows which books the family has been in together. It never shows chapters or who, and each person turns it on.

---

## Under 13: child notebooks

[Child notebooks](/add-ons/family/child-notebooks/) are coming soon, for children under 13 in the United States. They work the other way round from a teen's account, because a parent sets them up and is in charge of them.

In a child notebook, <mark>you can read your child's notes</mark> and download a copy. You choose the translations and chapters they read. Only you can see the notebook. If you approve your church's kids ministry reading, ministry leaders still can't see your child's notes or profile. Nothing in it is public, and there are no ads, messages, purchases or AI.

The verse stickers your child puts on verses stay private to them in the app. They reach you only in the full backup.

What we collect and why is in our [children's privacy policy](/privacy/children/). [Setting up a child notebook](/blog/set-up-a-child-notebook) covers how a parent gives consent.

---

## When a child turns 13

Nothing changes on its own. A child notebook stays yours to manage. From 13, your child can have their own Harvous account and join your family as a teen, with the progress-only view above. Moving a notebook into a teen's own account isn't available yet.

To start a family, see [Start a family in Harvous](/blog/start-a-family-in-harvous).
